## How sure are these numbers?

Memory figures come by formula from real GGUF file sizes, each file's header and each model's configuration, so they carry no error band. For the four distills, speeds are calibrated estimates, ±12% with the model fully on an NVIDIA card and ±20% with partial offload. CanRun's data attaches no public benchmark to these DeepSeek models, so no cell is measured. That includes the 32B's Runs well on the RTX 3090<Expect model="deepseek-r1-distill-32b" gpu="rtx-3090" verdict="A" confidence="calibrated" /> and the partly offloaded 14B's Runs slowly on the RTX 4060.<Expect model="deepseek-r1-distill-14b" gpu="rtx-4060" verdict="B" mode="partial-offload" confidence="calibrated" />

Every DeepSeek V4 Flash cell is a theoretical estimate (±30%).<Expect model="deepseek-v4-flash-0731" gpu="apple-m3-ultra" ram="256" fits="true" confidence="theoretical" /> Its compressed attention and the hyper-connections that mix each layer's input add work that CanRun's speed formula does not count. For the one public result from a current llama.cpp build, an M2 Ultra run, CanRun's estimate was about a quarter higher than the measured speed. In an August build on the same Mac, llama.cpp slowed by about a fifth between an empty context and a 30k one, while CanRun's estimate barely moves, so its long-context cells are optimistic. Long prompts slow down even more: in that August run, reading a prompt after 30k tokens of context went at about a quarter of the speed on an empty context, which CanRun's prompt processing estimate does not reflect. Builds from before August 2026 were much slower on Macs.

The 1.5× factor for thinking models is a fixed policy and the least certain part. Several cells sit right at the stricter line, where a few percent of real speed moves them a level: the 32B on the RTX 3090 against the RTX 4090, the 14B at Q4_K_M against IQ4_XS on the RTX 5060 Ti 16GB, DeepSeek R1 0528 Qwen3 8B on the RTX 4060, and the 32B at 32k with q8_0 on the RTX 3090.

All verdicts assume Windows with the card driving the display, 32GB of DDR5-5600 dual-channel system RAM, an 8k context, an f16 KV cache, one model loaded and one request at a time. Ollama's library files differ slightly from the community GGUFs CanRun uses, so `ollama ps` is the ground truth on your PC. The full R1, V3 and V4-Pro are outside CanRun's calculation.

## FAQ

### Can I run the full DeepSeek R1 on my PC?

Not with the 8GB to 32GB graphics cards in this guide, and CanRun does not calculate it. Its model card lists 671B total parameters with 37B activated, about twenty times the 32B distill. The full-size DeepSeek model CanRun does calculate is V4 Flash 0731, and it needs a large-memory Mac or a lot of system RAM: its MXFP4 file fits fully on a Mac Studio with M3 Ultra and 256GB,<Expect model="deepseek-v4-flash-0731" gpu="apple-m3-ultra" ram="256" fits="true" mode="full-gpu" /> and its Q2_K file fits with an RTX 5090 and 128GB of RAM, keeping most experts in RAM.<Expect model="deepseek-v4-flash-0731" gpu="rtx-5090" ram="128" quant="Q2_K" fits="true" mode="cpu-moe" /> The small distills are what a typical home PC runs.

### Is a DeepSeek R1 distill the same model as DeepSeek R1?

No. A distill is a Qwen or Llama model fine-tuned on samples that R1 generated, and it keeps its base model's architecture: Qwen2.5 for the 14B and 32B, Llama 3.1 for the Llama 8B and Qwen3 for the 0528 8B.

### What GPU do I need for the DeepSeek R1 32B distill?

A 24GB card such as the RTX 3090 or RTX 4090 holds Q4_K_M at 8k fully on the GPU.<Expect model="deepseek-r1-distill-32b" gpu="rtx-3090" fits="true" mode="full-gpu" /><Expect model="deepseek-r1-distill-32b" gpu="rtx-4090" fits="true" mode="full-gpu" /> On the 16GB RTX 5060 Ti, only the Heavy loss Q2_K file fits fully.<Expect model="deepseek-r1-distill-32b" gpu="rtx-5060-ti-16gb" quant="Q2_K" fits="true" mode="full-gpu" /> For 32k, the RTX 3090 needs a q4_0 KV cache to stay on the GPU, and then it Runs well.<Expect model="deepseek-r1-distill-32b" gpu="rtx-3090" ctx="32768" kv="q4_0" verdict="A" mode="full-gpu" /> The 32GB RTX 5090 holds 32k with an f16 cache and Runs great.<Expect model="deepseek-r1-distill-32b" gpu="rtx-5090" ctx="32768" verdict="S" mode="full-gpu" />

### Why does DeepSeek R1 seem to forget my question in Ollama?

Most likely its reasoning filled Ollama's default context. <RuntimeFact id="ollama-default-ctx" />. A distill can write a long chain of reasoning before it answers, and once the context is full, the start of the conversation drops out. Raise `OLLAMA_CONTEXT_LENGTH` as shown above. On an 8GB card such as the RTX 4060, pair 16k with a q8_0 KV cache; DeepSeek R1 0528 Qwen3 8B then stays fully on the GPU and Runs great.<Expect model="deepseek-r1-0528-qwen3-8b" gpu="rtx-4060" ctx="16384" kv="q8_0" verdict="S" mode="full-gpu" />

### Can I turn DeepSeek R1's thinking off?

In Ollama, yes: run `ollama run deepseek-r1 --think=false`, or type `/set nothink` in a chat. DeepSeek's January card warns that skipping the thinking can hurt the answers, though. CanRun still applies the thinking thresholds to these models, and `--hidethinking` only hides the reasoning.
